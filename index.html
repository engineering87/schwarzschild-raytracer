What is computed exactly

Each pixel casts one ray backwards in time from a static observer at Schwarzschild radial coordinate r. Because the Schwarzschild metric is spherically symmetric, every null geodesic stays in the plane spanned by the observer position and the ray direction, so the trajectory reduces to one ordinary differential equation in u = 1/r:

d2udφ2 +u = 3(GM/c2)u2

integrated with fixed-step RK4. This is the exact null geodesic equation of the Schwarzschild solution, not a weak-field approximation. The weak-field limit α=4GMc2b is recovered automatically for large impact parameter.

  • A ray reaching r < 2M is captured and rendered black. This produces the shadow, whose critical impact parameter is bcrit=33M.
  • Higher-order images (the photon ring, and the disk seen over and under the hole) appear because rays can wind past the photon sphere at r = 3M. Nothing is faked.
  • Initial conditions use the local orthonormal frame of the static observer, including the factor 1−2M/r on the radial momentum component, so the field of view is the one an observer at that radius would actually measure.

The disk is a geometrically thin, optically thick, equatorial ring of matter on circular Keplerian orbits. The frequency shift for that flow is

g= 1−3M/r 1−Ωλ Ω=M/r3

where λ=Lz/E is the photon's conserved axial impact parameter, obtained from the same integration. Observed intensity follows Iobs=g4Iem, which is Liouville's theorem applied to the invariant Iν/ν3. Doppler beaming and gravitational redshift are the two factors of that single expression and can be switched independently.

The radial temperature profile is the standard thin-disk law with a torque-free inner boundary,

T(r) ∝ [ 1r3 (1−rin/r) ]1/4

which peaks at r=4936rin. Colour comes from a blackbody at the observed temperature gT, and brightness from the Stefan-Boltzmann scaling of that same observed temperature.

What is approximated or invented

Read this before using any frame as evidence of anything.

  • Non-rotating hole only. This is Schwarzschild, spin a = 0. There is no frame dragging, no ergosphere, no Kerr ISCO shift. A real astrophysical hole almost certainly spins.
  • Colour temperature is a display choice. A real disk around a stellar-mass hole peaks in soft X-rays (10⁷ K) and around a supermassive hole in the ultraviolet. Nothing there is visible. The temperature slider maps the physical profile shape onto the visible band so that the shape and the relative shifts can be seen.
  • No radiative transfer. The disk emits as an opaque blackbody surface. There is no Comptonisation, no corona, no synchrotron, no absorption along the path, no plasma, no jet.
  • Turbulence texture is decorative. The banding is procedural noise sheared by the real Keplerian differential rotation. The shear is physical; the pattern is not a magnetohydrodynamic solution.
  • The star field is procedural, not a real catalogue. Its lensing and the Einstein rings are real consequences of the integration, but the stars themselves are invented.
  • Numerical truncation. Rays are declared escaped at a finite radius, so the residual deflection beyond it is dropped. The error is of order M/r_escape, roughly one degree at the default setting. Raising the escape radius and step count reduces it.
  • The lensing slider below 1.0 is not physics. It scales the 3Mu² term for comparison only. Only 1.0 is the Schwarzschild solution; 0.0 is straight-line optics.
Provenance

Every line of this simulation, the offline reference renderer, the verification scripts, and the documentation were produced by Anthropic Claude Opus 5 in a single session on 13 August 2026, from a one sentence brief that specified no metric, no integration scheme, and no verification strategy.

The repository states this plainly because it is the reference point of a comparison study across models. The full record, including what was verified, what was not, and the mistakes corrected along the way, is in docs/PROVENANCE.md.

WebGL 2 is not available

This simulation integrates a differential equation per pixel on the GPU and needs a WebGL 2 context. Enable hardware acceleration in your browser settings, or open the page on a machine with a GPU driver that supports WebGL 2.
